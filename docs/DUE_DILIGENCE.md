# Acquisition and Due Diligence

## Founder-led provenance

AICore is an independently developed personal project with direct founder involvement across product definition, software development, documentation, brand direction, and commercial strategy.

This provides prospective buyers with a direct line to the person who holds the project knowledge and can support an efficient evaluation and transition process.

## Public and confidential layers

The public showcase explains the product category, market value, project structure, commercial possibilities, and trust principles. Deeper product materials are reserved for qualified parties under appropriate confidentiality arrangements.

## Materials available for qualified review

| Evaluation area | Available perspective |
|---|---|
| Product | Vision, positioning, use cases, customer value, and portfolio fit |
| Software asset | Private project body, development history, and product evolution |
| Intellectual property | Ownership discussion, asset scope, brand materials, and transfer options |
| Platform strategy | Cross-platform direction, ecosystem role, and partner opportunities |
| Product experience | Demonstration and explanation of representative user journeys |
| Commercialization | Acquisition, licensing, enterprise, OEM, and partnership pathways |
| Transition | Founder knowledge transfer, product briefing, and continuity planning |

## Suggested engagement process

1. Initial strategic discussion and buyer-fit assessment
2. Mutual confidentiality arrangement
3. Controlled product and asset review
4. Commercial structure and transition planning
5. Definitive agreement and agreed knowledge transfer

## Acquisition scope

Subject to negotiated terms, an acquisition discussion can cover the AICore name, brand assets, software intellectual property, product documentation, commercial materials, and founder-supported transition knowledge.

## Credibility principles

- Public claims remain focused on product and market value.
- Confidential evidence is presented directly to qualified parties.
- Project history provides a traceable development narrative.
- The founder can explain product decisions and support knowledge transfer.
- Commercial scope and rights are documented through the transaction process.
